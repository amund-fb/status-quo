■ AI-Native Consultancy Plays

OpenAI Deployment Company: $4B, TPG/Bain/McKinsey, and 150 forward-deployed engineers from Tomoro

OpenAI launched the OpenAI Deployment Company on May 12, backed by $4B from TPG, Advent, Bain Capital, Brookfield, Bain & Company, Capgemini, and McKinsey. The company simultaneously acquired Tomoro — an applied AI consultancy with clients including Tesco, Virgin Atlantic, and Supercell — adding 150 forward-deployed engineers to the team. The model is explicitly boutique: small pods embedded into organizations to redesign workflows around AI. This is the most direct competitive challenge yet to independent AI-native firms.

OpenAI's FDE model and client list (consumer brands, airlines, games) maps precisely onto the same segments small AI-native operators target. The competitive response is differentiation on depth and speed, not breadth: proprietary client data, sector-specific playbooks, and delivery cadences that a corporate JV cannot match at small account sizes.

▲ Analyst Note

The interesting thing about a $4B consulting JV is that it confirms the thesis while threatening to commoditise it. When the largest AI lab in the world decides the future of enterprise AI is small embedded pods — not licences, not dashboards, not transformation programs — that is a structural admission. The pod model works. The question is whether it scales. Large organisations are structurally incapable of moving at boutique speed. The incentive structures are wrong, the approval chains are too long, and the institutional memory is too distributed. The FTI data makes the same point from a different angle: nearly every PE portfolio AI pilot fails to reach production. Not because the technology is wrong. Because the organisation around it is. The firms that will win are the ones already in production — not the ones announcing it. The gap between those two groups is compounding every week.
